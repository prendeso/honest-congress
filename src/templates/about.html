About & Methodology

Honest Congress reads the financial disclosures that members of Congress are required to file, and publishes what the filings say alongside patterns in them that may be worth a closer look. Every number here comes from an official, public-domain source: the House Clerk, the Senate eFD system, the congress-legislators dataset, the SEC, the FEC, the Senate LDA, USASpending and Congress.gov.

A finding is not an accusation

An “anomaly” on this site is the output of an automated rule applied to disclosure data. It means a pattern in the filings met a stated condition — a trade near a donation, a filing past its deadline, an unusually large transaction. It is not an allegation of wrongdoing, insider trading, a conflict of interest, or any breach of law or ethics rules, and it should not be reported as one. Many findings have ordinary explanations the filings do not record: trades made by a spouse or an adviser, assets held in managed accounts, or plain coincidence.

What the data can and cannot show

  • Amounts are ranges. Disclosures report value bands such as $1,001–$15,000, never exact amounts or share counts. Figures here carry those ranges rather than inventing a precise number.
  • Dates are as filed. A disclosure can arrive weeks after the trade it reports, and filing dates are not trade dates.
  • Some filings cannot be read. Filings are PDFs and web forms, and some are scanned paper with no text at all. Each filing carries a parse confidence score, and a member whose filings could not be read may show few findings for that reason alone. The legibility index says how much of each member's record was readable.
  • No price data. Nothing here measures trading returns or claims a member “beat the market”.

How findings are tested

Running many rules against hundreds of people produces some matches by chance alone. The detectors that ask a timing question — a trade close to a donation, a lobbying filing, a contract award, a bill — are tested against a null model: the member's own trading calendar is shifted in time many times to see how often the same number of coincidences occurs by chance. The results are then corrected for multiple comparisons (Benjamini–Hochberg), and by default only findings that survive that correction are shown.

Surviving that test means the timing is unlikely to be chance. It does not mean the member acted on any information, or that anything improper happened.

The other detectors measure a size — how late, how large, how concentrated — and have no chance model to test against. They are ranked against other findings of the same kind instead, and are marked as untested rather than as having passed anything.

Detectors that are switched off

Several detectors are disabled, either because their output could not be defended with the data available or because nobody has yet reviewed what they say about the current corpus. A disabled detector publishes nothing. The reasons for each are recorded in the project's decision log.

Corrections

If something on this site misstates what a filing says — a trade attributed to the wrong person, a misread amount or date, a finding that rests on a parsing error — please report it. Include the page address and, if you can, the filing's document ID.

Report a correction — corrections are published in the open, and a confirmed error is fixed in the pipeline so it cannot recur.

Source code

The code that produces every number on this site is public at github.com/prendeso/honest-congress, under the MIT licence.
